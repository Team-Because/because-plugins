# Studio taste · what Team Because accepts and rejects

Read at intake, before any pitch. Every line came from a real rejection or approval on a client film.
"The person" below is whoever asked for the video. A new rejection is sent with the feedback command so the whole team learns it.

## Direction (decided before anything is built)
1. "Motion video" means motion graphics built in code: shape, type, light, springs. Generated live action only when the person says film, ad or live action. Ask before switching medium.
2. "Wild" means a story with a character: an illustrated world, a mascot with emotions, an arc (day to night, problem to fix). A fancier infographic (teardown, counters, frame-break) reads as "very normal motion graphics". This holds for explainers and product news too. It is the bar, not proof the studio can hit it: see line 11.
3. Pick references by craft ceiling, never by topic. A food brand does not need a recipe video. Lead with the boldest technique, then map the brand's idea onto it.
4. Premium is the floor for client work, even when the style reference is lo-fi. Cute-but-crude fails.
5. A mascot or character is a design decision: 2-3 concept stills, the person picks, then rig. Never polish one direction for 3 rounds first.
6. "Like brand X for occasion Y": study X's own Y work first. Festival icon parades (diyas, garba sticks, rangoli for their own sake) are the cliche. The product becomes the festive object, with restraint.
7. A product inside a real scene must be plausible there. Nobody carries a wire coil at a Garba. If it cannot live in the scene, carry it graphically, built from the product's form.
8. A style reference sets the look, never the ambition. If it conflicts with "go all in", ask.
9. Borrow a reference's technique, never its colours: the client's brand colours always win.
10. The viewer knows within 3 seconds what the subject is and what each shape stands for. Fast is fine; disorienting is not.
11. Illustrated characters and crowds have read as childish so far. Accepted so far: the real product in premium light, type and springs. Lead with that; a character concept carries a one-line ceiling warning.
12. Never hand over a list of weaknesses you can fix yourself. Fix them, re-check, then show the film; only list what needs the person's call or money they have not approved.

## Craft (checked by tools/check.mjs where marked)
13. A reference's defining rule is a hard constraint, measured, never approximated. Match-cut circles: every circle sits inside one fixed zone, same centre, same size, never bigger; only flat, truly round objects qualify.
14. Nothing cut by the frame edge unless the shot list says so, in every format. [check: edges]
15. Moves have weight: closed-form springs, visible squash and stretch on characters, the camera follows the subject's rest on its own soft spring, never the bounce. [check: jolt via film.smooth]
16. Every sound tied to a motion lands within 1 frame of it, from one timing table. [check: sync]
17. The first frame is the thumbnail: it shows the subject. [check: frame0]
18. Something new every 2-4 s. A hold is allowed only at the final beat. [check: dead]
19. Text is readable at phone size; Reels UI covers the bottom 20%, so captions sit in the top band or mid-frame. [check: size]
20. Real product UI and real logos only. A generated image will add or remove light, but will not move a part or keep a logo exact.

## Sound and voice
21. Premium briefs get restrained sound: sub hits, ticks, real-sounding timbres, space. No novelty effects (Shepard tones, cartoon risers, obvious synth sweeps).
22. Voice, music and look are the person's call: offer an A/B, keep the current default, never mark yours "recommended" off a transcript check.
23. A person's photo keeps its expression and look in every generated version; a likeness photo gives identity only, never its background.

## Working with the person
24. Two touches: they pick a concept, they review the film. Everything between is yours, checked before they see it.
25. Plain words in questions. "Route 1 vs route 2" loses people; "site material + AI shots + code" does not.
26. If you can capture or generate it with the tools here, do it; do not turn it into a question.
27. Stills come from the engine first (free). Generated stills and clips go through Higgsfield, with the price named and a yes before each job.
